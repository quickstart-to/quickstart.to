# Editorial workflow

Use this for new guides, substantial revisions, and requested editorial reviews. The quality criteria live in [content-guide.md §8](content-guide.md#8-editorial-acceptance); this file explains how to apply them. For a review, start with the current rendered article and record passage-level findings before editing. Reopen only the research and writing affected by those findings; a review does not require a full rewrite. Keep the working record in the topic's existing research dossier, not in the published article.

## Before research: define the contribution

Read the topic's designated reference article and its editorial notes. Record its path/revision and the qualities to retain, along with known weaknesses. A reference is a comparison point, not proof of approval or a layout to copy. When none exists, use the content guide directly; do not invent one or block work awaiting approval.

Write a short brief: the reader's starting situation, the decision this chapter resolves, the usable output, and what it adds beyond the quickstart or adjacent chapters. Map the decisive questions to evidence or a worked demonstration. Identify the assumption most likely to invalidate the recommendation. This is a coverage check, not a source-count target.

## Before drafting: check the decisive questions

For each decisive question, identify suitable evidence, the intended reasoning/example, and any unresolved gap. In a comparison, examine the favored option's existing workaround, costs and disconfirming evidence as seriously as the rejected options. Unknowns are not advantages. Distinguish a reason to investigate cheaply from a reason to build or buy. Equal scrutiny does not require identical source counts or a numeric score.

Check whether the topic's defining conditions actually affect the choice. Where audience, region, language, resources or operating context matter, trace their effect on an input, trade-off or next action; adding those words to a generic example is insufficient. Reusing a case is useful only when this chapter examines a new decision in sufficient depth.

Continue research when a missing premise prevents a defensible choice. Do not move the central unanswered question into “future work” and call the chapter complete. A bounded hypothetical can demonstrate a method with stated assumptions and consistent consequences; it cannot establish real demand. Changes to the promised scope must remain compatible with the maintainer's request.

## Compose the explanation

Develop the work from inputs through alternatives, choice, result, and a condition that would change it. Show at least the consequential rejected option where a choice is being taught. Do not translate this sequence into compulsory section headings or repeated forms.

Assign media a job next to the relevant question. An illustration can establish a situation; a diagram explains relationships; a working example lets readers change an input and observe a consequence; a video adds an original demonstration or voice. Native disclosures are useful reading controls but do not by themselves demonstrate a causal relationship. Select formats for the explanation, with no image, video, case, or word quota. Keep essential content available without playback or scripting.

## After drafting: make a separate rejection pass

Read the rendered article as a reader, without using the implementation checklist as evidence of quality. Compare the promised contribution and the reference article. Look for a concrete reason to return the draft:

- Does a key choice still lack its inputs, alternative, reason, or consequence? Did the preferred option escape the scrutiny applied to its competitors?
- Is the chapter repeating the quickstart in longer form? Could its advice be transplanted to an unrelated topic?
- Does a case show what changed the author's decision, or merely decorate a conclusion?
- Do diagrams expose relationships and interactive examples expose consequences? Is the surrounding prose repetitive?
- Where does reading become tiring, overly cautious, mechanical, or dependent on an external link? Remove repeated decisions across prose, figures and final records; keep each occurrence only when it adds explanation, navigation or a usable output. Translate internal review language into the reader's actual next action.

Record actionable findings with the affected passage/revision, reader impact, evidence and repair. Prioritize errors and unsupported inferences over optional enrichment; do not recast taste as a factual defect. Re-read repaired passages in context, including related figures and the final choice. If no material issue remains, justify that conclusion with specific passages and the worked output; do not invent faults to fill a quota. Unresolved issues central to the promise keep the article in revision. A draft can be shared for review without being described as editorially complete.

## Report three separate results

1. **Evidence:** claims actually checked, dates, source fit, and remaining uncertainties.
2. **Editorial:** added reader capability, complete worked reasoning, comparison with the reference, rejection findings and repairs, residual limitations. Self-review is not independent reader acceptance.
3. **Technical:** build/content checks, actual asset delivery, links, narrow-screen reading, keyboard interaction, boundary/reset states, and static fallbacks as applicable.

A technical pass cannot override an editorial failure. Do not present successful rendering or a list of additions as proof of a finished article. Preserve actual reader feedback and revise the topic's reference notes when it changes the standard.
